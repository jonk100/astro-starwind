# re:MindMatters — Tagging System & Article Taxonomy Specification

## Core Strategy

To maintain strong search engine optimization (SEO), clean user navigation, and meaningful related-post recommendations, re:MindMatters operates on a **strictly consolidated, canonical tagging system**.

We completely prohibit ad-hoc tag creation, inconsistent casings, redundant parent-child tags, or subjective tone descriptors.

---

## 1. The 4-Category Tag Model

Every article or guide should have between **3 and 5 tags**, mapped exclusively across the following four categories:

| Category | Purpose | Max Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Topic** | Core well-being domain or subject | 1–2 | `mental-health`, `self-care`, `mindfulness` |
| **Condition** | Direct emotional/mental state or challenge | 0–2 | `anxiety`, `burnout`, `depression` |
| **Technique** | Actionable somatic or cognitive method/tool | 0–3 | `meditation`, `journaling`, `breathing-exercises` |
| **Meta** | Specific content format or publication series | 0–1 | `guide`, `explainer`, `song-sunday` |

---

## 2. Canonical Tag Rules

### Rule 1 — All Lowercase
Tags must consist strictly of lowercase characters. No capitalization or camelCase.
- **Good:** `self-care`, `cognitive-behavioral-therapy`
- **Bad:** `Educational`, `CBT`, `SMART-goals`

### Rule 2 — Consistent Hyphenation
Multi-word tags must be separated by standard hyphens (`-`). No spaces or underscores.
- **Good:** `mind-body-connection`, `goal-setting`, `boundary-setting`
- **Bad:** `self care`, `well being`, `goal setting`

### Rule 3 — Prefer Full Names Over Acronyms
To aid clear communication and semantic SEO clusters, always expand acronyms into their full name equivalents.
- **Good:** `cognitive-behavioral-therapy`, `post-traumatic-stress-disorder`, `seasonal-affective-disorder`
- **Bad:** `CBT`, `PTSD`, `SAD`

### Rule 4 — Avoid Redundant Parent-Child Tags
Never double-tag an article with both broad and narrow versions of the same concept. Pick the single most specific tag.
- **Bad:** `mental-health`, `mental-wellness`, `well-being` simultaneously.
- **Good:** `mental-health`

### Rule 5 — Substance Over Tone
Tags must describe the concrete subject matter of the article, not its tone or structural intention.
- **Bad:** `educational`, `tips-advice`, `strategy`, `awareness`, `development`
- **Good:** `anxiety`, `grounding-techniques`, `resilience`

## 4. Historical Tag Merging Dictionary

When editing legacy articles, merge historical categories into their canonical targets:

- **Merge into `mental-health`:**
  - `mental wellness`, `mental well-being`, `wellness`, `well-being`, `emotional well-being`, `emotional health`
- **Merge into `self-care`:**
  - `healthy habits`, `balance`, `positive mindset`, `positivity`
- **Merge into `stress-management`:**
  - `stress reduction`, `coping strategies`, `relaxation`
- **Merge into `relationships`:**
  - `relationship health`, `positive relationships`, `trust`, `dialogue`, `active listening`
- **Merge into `exercise`:**
  - `fitness`, `physical activity`, `mood improvement`
- **Merge into `nutrition`:**
  - `diet`, `healthy eating`, `balanced diet`, `relationship with food`

## 5. Strong Editorial Enforcement Rules

1. **Maximum 5 Tags:** No article may have more than 5 tags. The ideal count is **3–5**.
2. **One Primary Topic:** Every article must contain exactly one dominant Topic tag to establish its main categorization.
3. **No Synonyms:** Do not allow parallel terms (e.g. `anxiety` and `stress`) if one term comprehensively covers the article's core state.
4. **Tags Must Be Reusable:** If a tag will not realistically apply to **3 or more** articles, do not create or use it.
5. **SEO & Navigation First:** Only use tags that users would intentionally browse or search for as an archive filter.
